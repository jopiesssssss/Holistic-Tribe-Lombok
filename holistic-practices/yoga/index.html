01 / The essentials

A little understanding.
A better first step.

Yoga brings together movement, breathing, attention and, in many traditions, a wider philosophy. A studio timetable shows only part of that world. The pace, teaching approach and room for adapting a posture can matter as much as the name on the class. You do not have to be flexible to begin.

A useful distinction

The label is a starting point

Two classes with the same name can differ in pace, heat and instruction. Read the class description and ask about the teacher’s approach.

A useful distinction

A prop is part of the practice

Blocks, straps and cushions can make a position more accessible. Using support does not make your practice less complete.

01 / Steady+

Hatha

A posture-based class that often allows time to settle between movements.

Read more ↗

Hatha is also a broad historical term. On modern timetables it often indicates a steadier pace, but the actual intensity depends on the teacher.

Before you book

Ask how much standing work and how long the holds are.

02 / Dynamic+

Vinyasa

A flowing sequence in which movement is coordinated with breathing.

Read more ↗

Sequences vary between teachers. Transitions can be a substantial part of the practice, so a beginner class can help you learn them without rushing.

Before you book

Look for an introductory or slow-flow class for your first visit.

03 / Quiet+

Yin

Mostly floor-based postures held for longer periods.

Read more ↗

Yin can feel physically and mentally demanding despite its slower pace. Props and a smaller range of movement can change the experience.

Before you book

Choose a manageable sensation; strong pain is a reason to stop.

04 / Quiet+

Restorative

Supported positions with blankets, bolsters or blocks and little movement.

Read more ↗

Restorative classes usually use props to make resting positions comfortable. This differs from long stretches that ask for sustained effort.

Before you book

Check which props are provided.

05 / Dynamic+

Ashtanga

A structured practice built around established sequences.

Read more ↗

A led class follows the teacher’s rhythm. A Mysore-style class usually lets students work individually with guidance; ask how newcomers are introduced.

Before you book

Confirm whether the class is led or Mysore-style.

06 / Steady+

Private & adapted yoga

A session arranged around your experience, interests and practical needs.

Private tuition can make space for questions and variations. A private class is not automatically a clinical treatment or rehabilitation programme.

03 / A little context

Know the words.
Understand the setting.

Where the practice comes from

Yoga has roots in South Asian philosophical and spiritual traditions. Contemporary studio classes often emphasise postures, while other traditions place greater emphasis on meditation, ethics, devotional practice or breath. These traditions should not be reduced to a single exercise format.

Asana+

A posture or seat; in a studio class, usually the physical positions.

Pranayama+

Yoga breathing practices. Ask which technique is included, especially if holds or forceful breathing are involved.

Shala+

A place for practice or teaching; it does not describe a particular yoga style.

Savasana+

A resting posture commonly used near the end of class. You can ask for another comfortable position.

04 / From reading to trying

Make your first visit your own.

Three questions to ask

  1. Is the class suitable for my experience, and are alternatives offered?
  2. Is this a drop-in studio class, a private appointment or part of a stay?
  3. Are mats and props included, and what is the cancellation policy?

You can ask these questions before booking. Clear answers make it easier to choose a session that fits your plans.

Care & expectations

Good to know

An appropriately adapted class matters if you have an injury, are pregnant, have recently had surgery or have a health condition. Discuss relevant needs with the teacher and a qualified health professional when appropriate. You can skip a posture or rest at any point.

Educational information. No guaranteed outcomes or substitute for professional care.

07 / A few final questions

Before you go.

Can a beginner join yoga in Lombok?+

Many venues offer classes for different levels. Check the individual description and tell the teacher it is your first class.

Should I choose a class near my accommodation?+

That is often practical. Lombok and the Gili Islands are separate destinations; check the location before building a day around a class.

Do all listed providers teach every style in this guide?+

No. The style descriptions explain yoga generally. Open a provider’s timetable to see what that provider actually offers.
