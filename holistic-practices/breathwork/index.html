01 / The essentials

A little understanding.
A better first step.

Breathwork is an umbrella term for deliberately attending to or changing breathing. Some sessions simply help you notice an ordinary breath. Others use counted rhythms, pauses or faster patterns. The technique and the way it is taught matter more than an ambitious session title.

A useful distinction

An intense sensation is not a target

Tingling, dizziness or emotional responses do not prove that a treatment is working or that a particular trauma has been released. Tell the facilitator when something feels uncomfortable.

A useful distinction

Research is method-specific

A controlled study of short daily breathing exercises found changes in mood and breathing rate. That result does not establish the same effects for every breathwork workshop or a single intensive journey.

01 / Gentle+

Breath awareness

Notice the breath without trying to change its depth or speed.

Read more ↗

This can be part of meditation, yoga or a standalone session. The focus is observation, with permission to use another point of attention if breath feels uncomfortable.

Before you book

Ask whether you can keep your eyes open or sit up.

02 / Gentle+

Slow, comfortable breathing

A guided rhythm that stays easy and unforced.

Read more ↗

A teacher may suggest a slower pace or comfortable exhale. More effort is not necessarily more useful; a forced deep breath can feel unpleasant.

Before you book

Choose a comfortable position and return to normal breathing if needed.

03 / Yoga traditions+

Pranayama

A broad family of breathing methods within yoga traditions.

Read more ↗

Methods differ: some use alternate nostrils, while others involve holds or forceful breathing. A familiar yoga label does not make every method equally suitable.

Before you book

Ask for the actual technique rather than only the Sanskrit name.

04 / Intensive+

Connected breathing journeys

Sustained connected patterns, often accompanied by music and facilitation.

Read more ↗

Some formats use faster or deeper breathing and can produce strong physical sensations. Their screening, support and stopping options deserve careful discussion beforehand.

Before you book

Ask about health screening, group size and how to pause.

03 / A little context

Know the words.
Understand the setting.

Where the practice comes from

Breath practices appear in multiple contemplative and movement traditions. Modern breathwork also includes recently developed methods. A workshop may borrow from several approaches; it is reasonable to ask the teacher to name the method and explain their training.

Pacing+

The speed or rhythm suggested during a breathing exercise.

Breath retention+

A pause after breathing in or out. It should be explained before a session.

Integration+

Time to rest, reflect or discuss the experience after a session.

Consent+

Your ongoing choice about participation, touch and whether to stop.

04 / From reading to trying

Make your first visit your own.

Three questions to ask

  1. What breathing pattern do you teach, and how intensive is it?
  2. What health screening do you use, and can I choose a gentler option?
  3. What support is available during and after the session?

You can ask these questions before booking. Clear answers make it easier to choose a session that fits your plans.

Care & expectations

Good to know

Intensive breathing and breath holds are not suitable for everyone. Discuss pregnancy, cardiovascular or respiratory conditions, epilepsy, panic and relevant mental-health concerns before booking. Seek individual clinical advice where needed. Never practise intensive breathwork while driving, swimming or in water.

Educational information. No guaranteed outcomes or substitute for professional care.

07 / A few final questions

Before you go.

Is breathwork always intense?+

No. Breath awareness and comfortable paced breathing can be gentle. Ask which method a session uses.

Must I continue if I feel uncomfortable?+

No. A facilitator should explain how to pause, return to ordinary breathing and ask for help.

Does a workshop guarantee an emotional breakthrough?+

No. Experiences vary, and dramatic reactions are not a measure of success.
