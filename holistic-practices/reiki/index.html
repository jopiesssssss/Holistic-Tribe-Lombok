01 / The essentials

A little understanding.
A better first step.

Reiki is a complementary practice in which practitioners place their hands lightly on or just above a person’s body. Practitioners commonly describe it in terms of energy. That is part of the tradition’s explanation, rather than an established scientific mechanism.

A useful distinction

A quiet experience can still vary

You may notice warmth, thoughts or rest, or nothing particularly unusual. None of these responses confirms a diagnosis or an energy blockage.

A useful distinction

Keep the claim separate from the experience

NCCIH reports that Reiki has not been clearly shown to be effective for a health-related purpose. You can choose an experience for personal reasons while retaining realistic expectations.

Hands-on session

Light hand placement, usually while you rest fully clothed.

02 / In person+

Hands-off session

Hands are held above or near the body without direct contact.

Read more ↗

This format may appeal if you prefer no touch. Check that the practitioner will keep to the agreed approach throughout.

Before you book

Ask explicitly for a no-touch session if you want one.

03 / Remote+

Distance Reiki

A practitioner offers a session without being physically present.

Read more ↗

This is offered within some Reiki traditions. Scientific evidence does not establish a remote energy-healing mechanism or treatment effect.

Before you book

Ask what the appointment involves and what follow-up is included.

03 / A little context

Know the words.
Understand the setting.

Where the practice comes from

Modern Reiki is commonly traced to Mikao Usui in early twentieth-century Japan. Schools and lineages developed as the practice spread. A practitioner should be able to explain which approach they teach without presenting lineage as proof of clinical effectiveness.

Lineage+

The teaching succession identified by a practitioner or school.

Attunement+

A ritual or initiation used in Reiki training traditions.

Energy+

A term in the tradition’s explanation; it is not interchangeable with a measured medical quantity.

Boundaries+

The agreement about touch, personal information and what a practitioner can responsibly offer.

04 / From reading to trying

Make your first visit your own.

Three questions to ask

  1. Will this be hands-on or hands-off, and how is consent checked?
  2. What training and experience do you have?
  3. What happens during the appointment, and what does it cost?

You can ask these questions before booking. Clear answers make it easier to choose a session that fits your plans.

Care & expectations

Good to know

Reiki should not delay diagnosis or replace established treatment. Be cautious about anyone who promises a cure, diagnoses an illness through energy readings or advises stopping prescribed care. Discuss your comfort, mobility and touch preferences with the practitioner.

Educational information. No guaranteed outcomes or substitute for professional care.

07 / A few final questions

Before you go.

Do I stay dressed?+

Sessions are usually fully clothed. Confirm the format when booking.

Is touch required?+

No. Some practitioners offer hands-off sessions; agree on the format beforehand.

Is Reiki a medical treatment?+

It is offered as a complementary practice. Evidence has not clearly established effectiveness for health conditions, and it should not replace appropriate care.
