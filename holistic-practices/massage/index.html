01 / The essentials

A little understanding.
A better first step.

Massage involves working with soft tissues through touch, movement and pressure. Spa menus can use familiar names in different ways, so ask what the treatment actually includes. A useful choice starts with your comfort, the therapist’s training and a clear agreement about the appointment.

A useful distinction

Consent continues throughout

You can change the agreed pressure, ask to skip a body area or stop. A professional therapist should explain draping and allow privacy when changing.

A useful distinction

A venue and a treatment are separate choices

A beautiful resort setting does not tell you which therapist or technique will be available. Confirm the menu, appointment length and access for outside guests.

01 / Gentle+

Relaxation & oil massage

An appointment with flowing strokes and pressure adapted to your preference.

Read more ↗

Menus may call this relaxation, Swedish-inspired or aromatherapy massage. Oils, scents and techniques vary by venue.

Before you book

Tell the therapist about fragrance sensitivities and preferred pressure.

02 / Focused+

Deep-tissue massage

More sustained work directed at selected areas of soft tissue.

Read more ↗

Deep pressure is not automatically better. A therapist should be willing to reduce pressure and explain their approach without treating pain as a requirement.

Before you book

Agree on how you will communicate if the pressure is too much.

03 / Spa traditions+

Balinese-style massage

A spa label often combining several manual techniques.

Read more ↗

Individual treatments can draw on different influences. The menu name alone does not establish the therapist’s exact method or qualification.

Before you book

Ask what the session includes, rather than relying on the name.

04 / Movement+

Thai-style bodywork

A format that may combine pressure with assisted positions or stretches.

Read more ↗

It is often performed clothed, sometimes on a mat. The amount of stretching and the treatment setup vary.

Before you book

Mention injuries and ask for changes before a stretch becomes uncomfortable.

05 / Focused+

Targeted or sports massage

An appointment organised around a selected area or activity.

Read more ↗

A sports label can describe many approaches. If you need assessment or injury rehabilitation, check the practitioner’s clinical qualifications separately.

Before you book

Explain whether you want general comfort or help with a diagnosed condition.

03 / A little context

Know the words.
Understand the setting.

Where the practice comes from

Massage and bodywork have developed in many cultures. Modern spa menus often bring several approaches together. Descriptions such as Balinese, Thai or Swedish are useful clues, but should be paired with a clear explanation of the individual treatment.

Draping+

The towel or sheet arrangements used to preserve privacy during a treatment.

Pressure+

How firmly the therapist works; it should be agreed and adjustable.

Bodywork+

A broad label for hands-on approaches; ask which method is meant.

Treatment time+

The hands-on portion of an appointment, which may differ from the total booking length.

04 / From reading to trying

Make your first visit your own.

Three questions to ask

  1. Which treatment and therapist would suit my preferences?
  2. How are pressure, privacy and touch boundaries agreed?
  3. Can outside guests book, and where is the actual spa entrance?

You can ask these questions before booking. Clear answers make it easier to choose a session that fits your plans.

Care & expectations

Good to know

Mention injuries, recent operations, pregnancy, blood-thinning medication and relevant health conditions before treatment. Strong massage may be inappropriate in some circumstances; seek individual advice if unsure. Do not use massage to postpone assessment of unexplained or worsening symptoms.

Educational information. No guaranteed outcomes or substitute for professional care.

07 / A few final questions

Before you go.

Does stronger pressure mean a better massage?+

No. Choose a level you can tolerate comfortably and ask for it to be adjusted.

Can I visit a resort spa without staying there?+

That depends on the venue. Check the access notes and book directly before travelling.

Will every listed spa offer every style here?+

No. The guide explains common menu terms. The provider’s own current menu determines the available treatments.
