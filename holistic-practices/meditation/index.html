01 / The essentials

A little understanding.
A better first step.

Meditation describes many practices for working with attention and awareness. Some return to one point, such as a sound or a comfortable breath. Others notice changing sensations, thoughts or feelings. A wandering mind is part of the experience; the invitation is to notice and return, without needing to achieve a special state.

A useful distinction

A comfortable position helps

Meditation does not require sitting cross-legged. A chair, supported seat or standing position can make participation easier.

A useful distinction

Mindfulness is broader than a class

Mindfulness often describes attending to present experience. It can be practised in meditation or in everyday activities, and is taught in different religious and secular contexts.

01 / Attention+

Focused attention

Return to a chosen point, such as a sound, visual object or breath.

Read more ↗

The point of focus gives the session a clear reference. If breath awareness feels uncomfortable, ask whether an external sound or a visual point is an option.

Before you book

Begin with a short guided session rather than a long silent sitting.

02 / Guided+

Body scan

Bring attention to different areas of the body in sequence.

Read more ↗

This may be taught sitting or lying down. You can skip an area or return to the room around you if inward attention feels difficult.

Before you book

Choose a position you can change easily.

03 / Movement+

Walking meditation

Attend to walking, contact with the ground and the surroundings.

Read more ↗

A walking practice can be slow and formal or more informal. It offers a movement-based alternative to extended sitting.

Before you book

Ask whether the route is shaded and accessible.

04 / Intention+

Loving-kindness

Use phrases or intentions of goodwill towards yourself and others.

Read more ↗

Often called metta, this practice has Buddhist roots. Teachers may offer a traditional or a secular introduction.

Before you book

You can adapt a phrase that does not feel natural.

05 / Guided+

Guided rest & yoga nidra

A teacher leads attention through a structured resting practice.

Read more ↗

Yoga nidra comes from yoga traditions and is often offered lying down. A guided rest session is not the same as a silent meditation retreat.

Before you book

Check the duration and whether mats or cushions are provided.

03 / A little context

Know the words.
Understand the setting.

Where the practice comes from

Meditation practices appear in many religious, philosophical and secular settings. Buddhist, Hindu and other traditions have distinct methods and aims. A contemporary class may use selected practices in a secular format; ask the teacher how they describe their approach.

Anchor+

A point you return attention to, such as sound or the feeling of your feet.

Mindfulness+

A way of attending to present experience; definitions vary across traditions.

Metta+

A Pali term often translated as loving-kindness or goodwill.

Guided practice+

A session in which a teacher gives spoken prompts during the practice.

04 / From reading to trying

Make your first visit your own.

Three questions to ask

  1. Is the session guided or silent, and how long are the sitting periods?
  2. Can I use a chair, keep my eyes open or change position?
  3. What support is available if the practice feels difficult?

You can ask these questions before booking. Clear answers make it easier to choose a session that fits your plans.

Care & expectations

Good to know

Meditation is not comfortable for everyone at every time. Anxiety or other difficult experiences can occur. Start with a manageable duration, stop if needed and seek suitable professional support for persistent distress. A teacher should respect your choice to use an external focus or leave a practice.

Educational information. No guaranteed outcomes or substitute for professional care.

07 / A few final questions

Before you go.

Am I doing it wrong if my mind wanders?+

No. Many practices involve noticing that attention has wandered and returning to the chosen focus.

Do I have to sit on the floor?+

No. Ask about chairs or another comfortable position.

Is every retreat a meditation retreat?+

No. Check the programme. Some yoga retreats include short meditation sessions, while intensive silent retreats are a different commitment.
